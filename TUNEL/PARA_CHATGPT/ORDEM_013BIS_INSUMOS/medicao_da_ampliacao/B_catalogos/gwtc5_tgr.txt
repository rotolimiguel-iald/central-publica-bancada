GWTC-5.0: Tests of General Relativity
The LIGO Scientific Collaboration, the Virgo Collaboration, and the KAGRA Collaboration
(Compiled: 22 July 2026)
ABSTRACT
The signals from the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA network of gravitational-wave (GW) detectors allow us to perform
sensitive tests of general relativity (GR) in the dynamical and strong-field regime of gravity. We present the results
of seven tests of GR using the observed binary signals in the fifth GW Transient Catalog (GWTC-5.0), i.e., up to
and including the second part of the fourth observing run (O4b). We restrict our analysis to the confident signals,
henceforth called events, observed by at least two detectors that have estimated false alarm rates ≤10−3 yr−1.
These include 72 events from O4b and five events from the first part of the fourth observing run that are now
analyzed due to their increased significance from updated search results, bringing the total number of events
for tests of GR in the cumulative GWTC to 168. After subtracting the best-fit waveforms, we find the residuals
are consistent with detector noise for all events considered. We also find no strong evidence for additional
polarizations beyond those predicted by GR. We perform tests of GW generation, improving the constraints on
deviations from the GR post-Newtonian coefficients by factors of 1.2 to 2.6. Finally, we find overall consistency
of the remnants with GR using both time- and frequency-domain methods. For GW240621 195059, postmerger
data are consistent with the dominant quadrupolar (ℓ= |m| = 2) mode of a Kerr black hole and its first overtone,
with spurious high-frequency content preventing a spectroscopic constraint of GR. In the frequency-domain
ringdown analysis, the GR prediction lies in the tails of the combined results, possibly due to the limited catalog
size. However, the combined results indicate improved consistency with GR over GWTC-4.0, owing to the
contribution of GW250114 with a network matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio of 76.9. Overall, we find no
evidence for physics beyond GR.
1. INTRODUCTION
Gravitational wave (GW) observations of compact binary
coalescences (CBCs) have opened a new regime for testing
gravity in the strong-field, highly dynamical sector. Since the
first detection of a merger involving two black holes (BHs)
(Abbott et al. 2016a), followed by observations of systems
containing neutron stars (NSs) (Abbott et al. 2017a, 2021a),
the population of detected GW signals (hereafter referred to
as events) has grown rapidly, enabling increasingly precise
tests of Einstein’s theory of general relativity (GR). These
observations of binary BH (BBH), binary NS (BNS), and NS–
BH binary (NSBH) mergers probe gravitational dynamics in
regimes that were previously inaccessible, complementing
precision tests of gravity performed in the Solar System (Will
2014), with binary pulsars (Kramer et al. 2021), through ob-
servations of stars orbiting Sgr A∗(Abuter et al. 2018; Do et al.
2019; Abd El Dayem et al. 2025), using BH shadow measure-
ments (Akiyama et al. 2019, 2022a,b), and from cosmological
observations (Ishak 2019; Ferreira 2019).

Analyses of the GW signals detected during the first three
observing runs of the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave
Observatory (LIGO) (Aasi et al. 2015) and Virgo observatory
(Acernese et al. 2015) have already provided a wide range of
tests of GR, including consistency checks of the waveform
morphology, constraints on parameterized deviations from GR
predictions, and studies of the properties of the remnant BH
(Abbott et al. 2016b, 2017b,c, 2019a,b, 2020c, 2021b, 2025).
To date, these tests have shown overall consistency with the
predictions of GR, simultaneously improving constraints on a
wide range of possible deviations from the theory.
The fourth observing run of the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA Col-
laboration (LVK) has further expanded the number and di-
versity of observed compact binaries. The first part of this
run already yielded a rich sample of binaries (Abac et al.
2025a, 2026a), including several events whose properties
are particularly relevant for testing gravitational dynamics.
These events were analyzed in detail in the GWTC-4.0 tests
of GR catalog papers (Abac et al. 2026b,c,d). For example,
the likely NSBH system GW230518 125908 provided strong
constraints on deviations in the inspiral dynamics (Abac et al.
2026c), while GW231226 101520 offered improved measure-
ments of the remnant BH properties and the post-merger sig-
nal (Abac et al. 2026d). The tests of GR on the two single-
detector events GW230529 181500 (Abac et al. 2024) and
GW230814 230901 (Abac et al. 2026e) are reported else-

The second part of the fourth observing run (O4b), from
2024 April 10 to 2025 January 28, has continued to expand
this sample and includes several events that provide especially
informative probes of strong-field gravity. The asymmetric,
high-spin binary merger GW241011 233834 is among the
first detections allowing us to probe a region of the binary
parameter space that has so far been sparsely populated by
observations, providing a valuable opportunity to test gravita-
tional dynamics in unequal-mass systems (Abac et al. 2025b).
Similarly, the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) observation
of BBH coalescence GW250114 082203 (henceforth short-
ened to GW250114; Abac et al. 2025c) has enabled partic-
ularly strong constraints on both post-Newtonian (PN) co-
efficients and quasi-normal mode (QNM) deviations (Abac
et al. 2026f). Detailed analyses of this event have yielded
stringent tests of the Kerr nature of BHs as well as Hawking’s
area theorem (Abac et al. 2025c, 2026f). We also include
GW240925 005809, a loud event observed for which the orig-
inal detector calibration was subsequently revised in the C01
recalibration (Abac et al. 2026g). Comparisons with astro-
physical calibration measurements show that the recalibrated
strain data are consistent with the expected detector response,
while remaining calibration uncertainties can be incorporated
through joint inference of calibration and source parameters.
These studies indicate that any potential biases are adequately
controlled, supporting the inclusion of this event in our analy-
ses. These notable events illustrate the increasing scientific
reach of GW observations as the detector network continues
to become more sensitive.
In this work, we analyze the GW signals that constitute the
fifth Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog (GWTC-5.0; Abac
et al. 2026h,i), combining the new detections from O4b with
previously observed events. We restrict ourselves to events
that pass the same selection criteria described in the previous
tests of GR papers (Abbott et al. 2025; Abac et al. 2026b)
of having an estimated false-alarm rate (FAR) ≤10−3yr−1
and having been observed by at least two detectors. Thus, of
the O4b candidates, this paper includes 72 new events. Of
the O4a candidates, this paper covers five additional events
that only passed the FAR threshold in the reanalyses carried
out with the PyCBC and GstLAL search pipelines using up-
dated configurations (Abac et al. 2026i), and thus were not
analyzed in the GWTC-4.0 tests of GR papers (Abac et al.
2026b,c,d). We also include the events from the previous ob-
serving runs: O1 (the first observing run, from 2015 Septem-
ber 12 to 2016 January 19; Abbott et al. 2016c), O2 (the
second observing run, from 2016 November 30 to 2017 Au-
gust 25; Abbott et al. 2019c), O3a (the first half of the third
observing run, from 2019 April 1 to 2019 October 1; Abbott
et al. 2021c, 2024), O3b (the second half of the third observ-
ing run, from 2019 November 1 to 2020 March 27; Abbott
et al. 2023), and O4a (from 2023 May 24 to 2024 January 16;
Abac et al. 2026a). Together, these observations provide the
largest sample of compact binary mergers yet used to test
gravitational dynamics.

Many of the tests covered have additional, narrower selection
criteria for choosing which events are relevant for them, based
on the supported physical parameters; these are described in
the respective sections.
While GWTC-4.0 tests of GR papers (Abac et al. 2026b,c,d)
reported results from a broader range of pipelines, the present
paper prioritizes tests that provide broad coverage of potential
deviations from GR, have well understood systematics, and
are expected to yield the most informative constraints with
the current set of events. Several analyses reported in earlier
catalogs are therefore not included in this paper due to tech-
nical reasons or to limit the scope of the paper, as detailed in
the following.
The inspiral–merger–ringdown (IMR) consistency test
(Hughes & Menou 2005; Ghosh et al. 2016, 2018) is not
included because Narayan et al. (2023, 2026) showed that
this test can exhibit spurious biases even for unlensed, qua-
sicircular binary signals in certain portions of the parameter
space. This is in addition to finding the expected biases when
applying this test and other ones to lensed or eccentric bi-
nary signals using an unlensed, quasicircular GR baseline.
The subdominant multipole amplitudes test (Puecher et al.
2022; Gupta et al. 2025) provides comparatively weak con-
straints for current data and can only be applied to a limited
subset of events, with the best constraint so far coming from
GW241011 233834, whose source was a asymmetric BBH
system (Abac et al. 2025b). The Test Infrastructure for GEn-
eral Relativity (TIGER; Li et al. 2012a; Agathos et al. 2014;
Meidam et al. 2018; Roy et al. 2026) post-inspiral analysis is
less straightforward to interpret than tests based on deviations
in individual PN coefficients. The multiparameter PN coeffi-
cients test using principal component analysis (Saleem et al.
2022; Mahapatra et al. 2025) is excluded to simplify the scope
of the paper, since it probes deviations in the inspiral dynam-
ics that are partially redundant with those constrained by the
other included parameterized PN deviation analyses. The spin-
induced moment tests (Krishnendu et al. 2017, 2019; Mehta
et al. 2023; Divyajyoti et al. 2024) only analyze a relatively
small subset of the events and also obtained the most strin-
gent constraints to date from GW241011 233834 (Abac et al.
2025b) due to the event’s high SNR, large primary spin, and
unequal mass ratio. The line-of-sight acceleration (Vijayku-
mar et al. 2023; Tiwari et al. 2026) analysis does not directly
probe deviations from GR and only a few events satisfy its
selection criteria. The test for dispersive propagation (Mir-
shekari et al. 2012; Baka et al. 2026) is currently investigating
correlations between the binary parameters and the modifica-
tions in the dispersion relation, as well as refining its event
selection criterion to select a subset of events that contribute
most significantly to the constraints. The spacetime symmetry
breaking test (Haegel et al. 2023) for anisotropic birefringent

the paper. The pyRing (Carullo et al. 2019) ringdown analysis

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2
discusses the events included, and details which tests were
performed for each. Section 3 describes the general methods
common between many of the tests. Section 4 describes the
residual test. Section 5 describes the tests for possible non-GR
polarizations. Section 6 describes the tests of modifications in
the GW generation from CBC systems, specifically tests of
parameterized deviations in individual PN coefficients with
TIGER, using the IMRPhenomXPHM SpinTaylor waveform
model, and the Flexible Theory-Independent (FTI) test, using
the SEOBNRv5HM ROM waveform model. In Section 7, we
discuss three ringdown tests of remnants of BBH mergers: the
ringdown analysis (RD) considering the post-inspiral signal in
the time domain (Section 7.1), pSEOBNR analyzing the entire
signal in the frequency domain (Section 7.2), and the QNM
rational filter (QNMRF) analysis applying a filter to determine
the QNMs present in the post-inspiral signal (Section 7.3).
Section 8 provides overall concluding remarks.

2. THE GRAVITATIONAL-WAVE EVENTS
The sample of signals analyzed in this work consists of
CBCs reported in GWTC-5.0 that satisfy the selection criteria
adopted for the tests of GR. Table 2 lists the events from O4b
that pass these requirements, while Table 3 shows additional
events from O4a that were not included in the GWTC-4.0
tests of GR but satisfy the same selection criteria when using
the reanalyzed PyCBC and GstLAL search results with up-
dated configurations (see Appendix A of Abac et al. 2026i).
The event GW240925 005809 (Abac et al. 2026g) is included
using the C01 recalibrated strain data, which corrects the
calibration error present in the observed data. Comparisons
with astrophysical calibration measurements indicate consis-
tency with the expected detector response, and the remaining
calibration uncertainty is incorporated through the standard
calibration-marginalization procedure. Combined with events
analyzed in earlier catalog studies, these observations provide
the dataset used in the tests presented in this paper.
The tables summarize several basic properties of each event.
For each signal we report the network matched-filter SNR
and the interferometers (IFOs) that contributed to the analysis
(H = LIGO Hanford, L = LIGO Livingston, V = Virgo). We
also include several quantities inferred from the parameter es-

et al. 2026h), namely the redshifted chirp mass (1 + z)M, the

3. METHODS FOR TESTING GR
3.1. Bayesian Inference
For each event, we analyze a short data segment d(t) around
the coalescence time and compare it with waveform predic-


=====PAGE 6=====
6
tions using a likelihood function p(d|θ, M), where M denotes
the assumed signal model and θ represents the associated pa-
rameters. Most of the analyses presented in this work rely on
the Bilby inference framework (Ashton et al. 2019; Romero-
Shaw et al. 2020), often supplemented by the BilbyTGR
extension designed for tests of GR (Ashton et al. 2025b). A
detailed description of the general Bayesian PE framework
used in the GWTC analyses is provided in the companion
methods paper (Abac et al. 2026j). In the analyses considered
here, the detector data are modeled as the sum of Gaussian,
stationary noise and a GW signal (Abbott et al. 2020a). Under
this assumption, subtracting the best-fit waveform from the
data should leave residuals consistent with Gaussian noise.
For a network of detectors, the likelihood is constructed as
the product of the individual detector likelihoods, assuming
statistical independence.
We adopt weakly informative priors p(θ|M) that are suf-
ficiently broad to encompass the region where the posterior
distribution has significant support. Uniform priors are typi-
cally used for most parameters, with ranges chosen to avoid
posterior probability accumulating near prior boundaries. The
priors used for the parameters describing compact binaries in
GR follow those adopted in the catalog analyses described in
Abac et al. (2026j). Note that we use the ΛCDM cosmologi-
cal model given in Abac et al. (2026h), where CDM refers to
cold dark matter. Priors for parameters describing deviations
from GR are specified independently for each individual anal-
ysis. Uncertainties in detector calibration are incorporated
through frequency-dependent amplitude and phase corrections
modeled as spline functions with Gaussian priors on their co-
efficients. These calibration parameters are marginalized over
during sampling following the procedure described in Abac
et al. (2026j).
The posterior distribution of the parameters is obtained
from Bayes’ theorem,
p(θ|d, M) = p(d|θ, M) p(θ|M)

In several tests, the evidence is used to compare different
signal models. For example, when evaluating a model that

In other analyses, deviations from GR are characterized
through posterior distributions of phenomenological parame-

posterior quantile of the GR prediction. When multiple devia-
tion parameters are considered simultaneously, we compute a
multidimensional quantile defined as the fraction of posterior
volume with probability density greater than or equal to that
of the GR value.
3.2. Hierarchical Inference
While the analyses described above infer parameters in-
dependently for each event, additional information can be
obtained by combining results across multiple detections. As
the number of observed GW events grows, combining in-
formation across multiple detections becomes increasingly
important for placing tight constraints on possible deviations
from GR. This can be done either by directly combining
posterior distributions for parameters assumed to be com-
mon across events, or more generally through hierarchical
inference, in which the distribution of parameters across the
population is modeled in terms of hyperparameters (Isi et al.
2019a; Mandel et al. 2019; Zimmerman et al. 2019).
For parameters describing effects that are expected to be
identical for all sources, the combined constraint is obtained
by jointly analyzing the measurements from all events. In
contrast, for parameters that may vary between individual
observations, such as phenomenological deviations in wave-
form coefficients, we infer the parameters by describing the
ensemble of measurements.
In practice, we model the distribution of deviation parame-
ters across events using a Gaussian population characterized
by a mean µ and standard deviation σ (Isi et al. 2019a). When
multiple deviation parameters are combined simultaneously,
as in the pSEOBNR analysis, the full covariance structure of
the population is inferred, including correlation coefficients
ρ between parameters (Zhong et al. 2024). The FTI, TIGER
and pSEOBNR tests employ hierarchical inference for com-
bining results. The set of GWTC-4.0 events included in the
hierarchical analyses, in addition to the new events listed in
Tables 2 and 3, is reported in the corresponding tables of Abac
et al. (2026c,d).
4. RESIDUAL TEST
The residual test checks for any excess coherent power that
remains in the detector network after the best-fit GR template
is subtracted out from the data. If GR correctly describes
the GW signal, the GR-based template would capture all
astrophysical features in the signal. In this case, the resulting
residual would be consistent with instrumental noise. We use
the same method as in previous analyses for this test (Abbott
et al. 2019b, 2021b, 2025; Abac et al. 2026b).
The noise in ground-based GW detectors arises from var-
ious sources (Aasi et al. 2015; Abbott et al. 2016d). The
detector noise is typically assumed to be stationary and Gaus-
sian, so the data time series d(t) is modeled as the sum of the
Gaussian noise n(t) and the GW model waveform h(t):

between the data d(t) and the template h(t).
Once the best-fit (maximum-likelihood) waveform hmaxL(t)
is obtained using a GR template—here IMRPhenomX-
PHM SpinTaylor (Pratten et al. 2021; Colleoni et al. 2025)
for the O4b events—the residual is calculated as r(t) = d(t) −
hmaxL(t). The residual r(t) should be consistent with noise if
hmaxL provides an accurate GR-based estimate of the signal.
We assess this by analyzing the residual with BayesWave algo-
rithm (Cornish & Littenberg 2015; Cornish et al. 2021, 2026).
BayesWave employs a template-independent model based on
Morlet–Gabor wavelets to characterize any coherent features
in the detector network. It produces a discretized probability
distribution over the wavelet parameter space, with each point
corresponding to a residual reconstruction with a well-defined
SNR. Any loud, multi-detector coherent features in the data
d(t) that are not captured by the GR-based model h(t) are re-
constructed. If the true underlying signal is well reconstructed
by the maximum-likelihood waveform hmaxL, the BayesWave
reconstruction of the residual SNR posterior will be consistent
with noise (Johnson-McDaniel et al. 2022).
The residuals are quantified by calculating the 90th
percentile of the network SNR distribution produced by
BayesWave, denoted SNR90. This means there is a 90% prob-
ability that, after subtracting hmaxL, the residual signal has
an optimal network SNR ≤SNR90. For the case of Gaussian
noise in the LIGO Hanford, LIGO Livingston, and Virgo net-
work, this value is typically ≲5 (Johnson-McDaniel et al.
2022).

5. TESTS OF POLARIZATION
Polarization tests (Wong et al. 2021) involve constructing
a linear combination of strain data, called the null stream, to
eliminate excess power and compare the residual data to noise,
allowing one to constrain possible non-tensorial polarizations
of GWs. The null stream is determined geometrically, relying
solely on the beam pattern function (Forward 1978; G¨ursel &
Tinto 1989; Nishizawa et al. 2009; Błaut 2012; Isi et al. 2015;
Isi & Weinstein 2017), which describes detector responses to
GWs, independent of the waveform morphology.
In the frequency domain, the observation model is ex-
pressed as

polarization modes at the geocentric time, and ˜n( f; ∆t) is the
detector noise, also time-shifted to the geocenter. Here the
elements of ˜d and ˜n represent the strain data and detector
noise for each individual detector, respectively (time-shifted
to the geocenter), so for an N-detector network, these are
N-dimensional vectors. ˜h(f) is a vector of the different polar-
ization modes, so an M-dimensional vector for M polarization
modes. α and δ denote the source’s right ascension and dec-
lination, ψ is the polarization angle, and ∆t = ∆t(α, δ, tevent)
represents the time delays from the geocenter to the detectors.
Since the beam pattern function varies negligibly over the

The TIGER analysis uses a waveform model that includes
spin precession, and therefore is not affected by the same
spin-precession related bias seen in the FTI analysis. For
GW241011 233834, TIGER finds all inspiral deviation pa-
rameters to be consistent with GR. This event provides the
strongest bounds in higher PN coefficients among the TIGER
analyses in GWTC-5.0. The event has a positive effective in-
spiral spin, χeff = 0.49+0.06
−0.07, which allows the binary to remain
in the inspiral regime up to higher orbital velocities compared
to a non-spinning binary. This follows from the spin contribu-
tions to the orbital frequency evolution, including spin–orbit
and spin–spin couplings, which modify the time spent in band
and the accumulated phase (Kidder et al. 1993). In particu-
lar, a positive χeff increases the signal duration relative to a
non-spinning binary from the same reference frequency. As
a result, higher-PN-order terms make a larger contribution to
the accumulated waveform phase. Spin effects also contribute
to the phasing, with the leading spin–orbit terms entering at
1.5PN order and the leading spin-spin terms entering at 2PN
order. The combination of high SNR, positive effective spin,
and measurable higher-order multipole moment content helps
reduce degeneracies between the binary parameters and the
inspiral deviation parameters, leading to stronger constraints,
particularly at higher PN orders. Evidence for spin precession
in this event can also contribute to this degeneracy breaking,
although we do not separately quantify its impact here.

A useful framework for interpreting agnostic PN deforma-
tion tests is the parameterized post-Einsteinian formalism,
which provides a systematic description of leading deviations
from general relativity in the gravitational waveform phase
and amplitude (Yunes & Pretorius 2009; Cornish et al. 2011).
In this approach, deviations are introduced as power-law cor-
rections at specific PN orders, allowing one to connect agnos-
tic constraints on PN coefficients to a broad class of modified
gravity theories.
To provide intuition for the meaning of the agnostic con-
straints on deviations in the PN coefficients we have obtained,
we thus follow the ppE formalism and perform event-by-event
illustrative mappings of the inferred PN deformation poste-
riors onto parameters appearing in selected modified gravity
theories, with the results given in Table 7. The example
theories considered are: scalar–tensor theories with an evolv-
ing scalar field (Jacobson 1999; Horbatsch & Burgess 2012),
Einstein–dilaton–Gauss–Bonnet (EdGB; Gross & Sloan 1987;
Kanti et al. 1996), dynamical Chern–Simons (dCS; Alexander
& Yunes 2009), and Einstein–Maxwell with BH charge.
These mappings should not be interpreted as theory-specific
statistical constraints, but rather as illustrative translations
of agnostic deviations into the language of particular mod-
els, obtained under simplifying assumptions (e.g., leading-
order modifications and no deviations in the merger–ringdown
phase).
Each mapping is performed independently for each GW
event using the corresponding posterior samples of both in-
trinsic binary parameters and the agnostic PN deformation
parameter, with consistent reweighting to account for prior
differences.
In order to map a PN parameter constraint to a parameter
in a given theory, we map use the posteriors of the PN coeffi-
cient constraint along with the intrinsic parameters for each
analyzed O4b event and the PN expressions derived to leading
order in Yagi et al. (2012a) for EdGB, Yagi et al. (2012a,b)
for dCS gravity, Khalil et al. (2018) for Einstein–Maxwell
with BH charge, and Horbatsch & Burgess (2012); Yunes et al.
(2016) for scalar–tensor theories with an evolving scalar field.
The mapping on theories we quote in Table 7 are the 90%
upper bound for the GW event from O4b that gives the tightest
translation on a given parameter. For comparison, we also
provide existing bounds on each theory. Current constraints on
scalar–tensor theories come from observations of the quasar
OJ 287 (Horbatsch & Burgess 2012). For EdGB gravity and
Einstein–Maxwell, the strongest bound arises from the low-
mass, long-inspiral signal GW230529 181500 (S¨anger et al.
2026). For dCS gravity, the current bound of 8.5 km comes
from combining NICER observations of a pulsar with the
NS tidal deformability inferred from the binary NS event
GW170817 (Silva et al. 2021).

7. RINGDOWN TESTS
The ringdown-based pipelines presenting results in this sec-
tion are ringdown (Section 7.1), pSEOBNRv5PHM (Section
7.2), and QNMRF (Section 7.3). The various analyses probe
the nature of the spacetimes of remnant BHs born from BBH
mergers. The perturbed remnant radiates away its excess en-
ergy in the form of GWs during the ringdown (post-inspiral–
merger) phase to reach a stable Kerr solution (Kerr 1963;
Press & Teukolsky 1973; Teukolsky 1973; Whiting 1989;
Buonanno et al. 2007). In the late stages of ringdown, the
dynamics of the remnant’s spacetime are governed by linear
perturbations, such that the emitted gravitational radiation
takes the form of superposed exponentially damped sinusoids
called QNMs when projected onto a basis of spin-weighted
spheroidal harmonics (Vishveshwara 1970; Teukolsky 1973;
Cunningham et al. 1978; Chandrasekhar 1983; Berti et al.
2026). The ringdown and QNMRF analyses adhere to this
perturbation theory-based QNM template for post-merger sig-
nals, while pSEOBNRv5PHM models the entire signal with
the transitions between pre- and post-merger portions of the
template informed by numerical relativity (NR). ringdown
and QNMRF employ time-domain likelihoods (though the
latter first applies frequency-domain filters to the data), while
pSEOBNRv5PHM operates in the frequency domain.
The complex spectrum of QNMs is determined solely by
the mass and spin of the remnant BH (Penrose 1969; Carter
1971; Hawking 1972; Robinson 1975; Penrose 2002); we
do not expect astrophysical BHs to be charged (Wald 1974;
Gibbons 1975; Blandford & Znajek 1977), and charges have
a demonstrably negligible effect in the ringdown for present
detector sensitivities (Carullo et al. 2022; Gu et al. 2024). Con-
sequently, resolving a single mode in the ringdown signals
of detected events with QNM models like those of ringdown
and QNMRF permits independent measurements of remnant
properties that can corroborate parameter estimates from IMR
analyses. Furthermore, through BH spectroscopy, multiple
resolved modes in a ringdown signal provide a direct probe of
the Kerr prediction for the nature of remnant BHs (Detweiler
1980; Dreyer et al. 2004; Berti et al. 2006, 2026). A comple-
mentary test is that of pSEOBNRv5PHM, which is itself a
coherent IMR analysis that constrains parameterized defor-
mations of the Kerr QNM spectrum within an NR-calibrated
mapping between remnant and binary properties.
The reliability of GR tests with BH spectroscopy hinges
on the assumption that the ringdown is consistent with lin-

ear perturbations on a Kerr background, and that a sum of
QNMs with constant amplitudes is an appropriate description
of the signal. This asymptotic regime is preceded by merger-
dependent initial conditions for QNM excitations (Berti &
Cardoso 2006; Albanesi et al. 2023; Lagos & Hui 2023;
Chavda et al. 2025; De Amicis et al. 2026), nonlinear dy-

The ringdown (Isi et al. 2019b; Isi & Farr 2021; Siegel et al.

more than one ringdown mode is resolvable in a given signal.

The analysis takes as input a handful of parameters drawn
from the posterior distribution of a full-signal analysis. These
include the geocenter time corresponding to the time of co-

time by truncating the strain data and excluding those earlier
samples from likelihood evaluation.
Our selection criterion for analysis intends for a given
signal to have a great enough post-peak optimal SNR, ρpp,
to resolve at least one long-lived mode and an additional
sub-dominant mode in the signal. To determine which events
meet this threshold, we compute the optimal SNR of the
truncated NRSur7dq4 waveform constructed from 500 pos-
terior sample draws, and we choose the events for which
the median ρpp is 10 or greater. This particular threshold
is motivated by ringdown analyses of GW150914 (Abbott
et al. 2016a,e), which find varying degrees of support for
an identifiable overtone in the postmerger signal (Giesler
et al. 2019; Isi et al. 2019b; Cotesta et al. 2022; Finch &
Moore 2022; Carullo et al. 2023; Isi & Farr 2023; Ma et al.
2023a; Correia et al. 2024); for a summary of these works,
see Berti et al. (2026). For all selected events, we analyze
their postmerger signals with the (2,2,0) and (2,2,0)+(2,2,1)

reports indicate the presence of a glitch in the Livingston data
near the time of the event, and default analyses adopt a low-

behavior suggests the presence of a residual in the data, which
the waveform model accommodates by shifting parameters

noise comes from analyses performed using a PSD estimated

posterior distributions for both the total mass and δ ˆf220 differ
significantly from those obtained with the default BayesWave
PSD based on on-source data, losing their bimodal structure
and instead peaking around the non-GR mode. Taken together,
these results point to non-stationary noise in the Livingston
detector as the most likely explanation for the observed fea-
tures. In light of these findings, our final results for this event
are obtained excluding the Livingston detector data.

7.3. The QNM Rational Filter analysis
The QNMRF (Ma et al. 2022, 2023a,b; Lu et al. 2025) ana-
lyzes postmerger signals from BBH systems to identify the
QNMs present and, if more than one mode is present, perform
BH spectroscopy. A test of GR via BH spectroscopy aims to
determine whether the observation of multiple QNMs in the
ringdown signal is consistent with the spectrum predicted for
a Kerr BH. In this context, the identification of a subdomi-
nant mode in addition to the dominant 220 QNM, along with
detector-frame remnant mass (1 + z)Mf and spin χf estimates
that agree with those from the full IMR analysis, constitutes a
self-consistency test of GR. In the remainder of this section
we denote the mass and spin pair as ϑf = {(1 + z)Mf, χf}. To
test for this self-consistency, the QNMRF applies filters in
the frequency domain to eliminate specific complex-valued
QNMs from the ringdown signal, and then compares the resid-
ual with colored Gaussian noise. For a ringdown model with
a chosen set of QNMs, a rational filter is constructed for a
given pair ϑf of remnant BH mass and spin. By applying
filters to the frequency-domain strain data and transforming

8. CONCLUSIONS
The second part of the fourth observing run of the LVK
network has substantially expanded the sample of GW sig-
nals from GWs. In this work we analyze 72 events detected
during O4b and five events from O4a, together with previ-
ously observed events from earlier observing runs that satisfy
the selection criteria adopted for tests of GR. This brings
the total number of analyzed events for tests of GR in the
cumulative GWTC to 168. These signals were subjected to
a set of seven complementary analyses probing different as-
pects of GW generation, propagation, and the properties of
the remnant BH.
Within the set of tests considered in this paper we find no
significant evidence for deviations from the predictions of
GR. The residual tests show that the reconstructed signals
leave noise-like residuals in the detector data, while no sig-
nificant evidence is found for additional polarization content
beyond the one predicted by GR. Additionally, we improve
the constraints on deviations from the GR PN coefficients by
factors of 1.2–2.6. Similarly, the ringdown analyses improve
the constraint on the dominant quadrupolar (ℓ= m = 2, n = 0)
mode by a factor of 1.95 and 1.48 for the fractional deviation
in the frequency and damping time, respectively. When events
are combined by multiplying likelihoods (hierarchically), the
frequency-domain ringdown analysis places the GR predic-
tion at the edge of the 98.1+1.9
−6.9% (98.7+1.3
−13.1%) credible region,
where the quoted intervals are obtained via bootstrapping to
account for the finite number of analyzed events. The agree-
ment with GR is improved relative to GWTC-4.0, primarily
due to the inclusion of GW250114. Furthermore, we infer a
nonzero amplitude of the (ℓ= m = 2, n = 1) overtone for
GW240621 195059, though content beyond the two-mode
template biases the inferred deviations from the Kerr spectrum
in the overtone. Moreover, we do not find evidence for ad-


=====PAGE 27=====
27
ditional subdominant ringdown modes in the events. Finally,
Tables 7 and 10 provide illustrative mappings of inspiral and
ringdown constraints onto parameters of selected modified
gravity theories.
In a few individual cases, the posterior distributions of devi-
ation parameters place the GR value outside the 90% credible
interval. Given the number of events analyzed, such occur-
rences are expected and do not constitute evidence for a break-
down of GR. The growing number of high-significance detec-
tions also highlights the importance of carefully accounting
for potential systematic effects in tests of strong-field gravity.
In particular, instrumental artifacts, non-Gaussian noise fea-
tures, and waveform-modeling uncertainties can influence PE
and may mimic apparent deviations from GR. Continued im-
provements in detector characterization, waveform modeling,
and analysis methodologies will therefore remain essential as
the sensitivity of the detector network increases and louder
signals become more common.
Several exceptional events from O4b have already mo-
tivated dedicated studies of strong-field gravity and BH
dynamics.
For example, the asymmetric high-spin bi-
nary GW241011 233834 (Abac et al. 2025b), the very
loud event GW250114 (Abac et al. 2025c, 2026f), as well
as GW240925 005809 and GW250207 11564 (Abac et al.
2026g) provide particularly stringent probes of relativistic
dynamics and the properties of the remnant BH. Such events
demonstrate the scientific potential of the current observing
run and motivate the continued development of increasingly
precise tests of gravitational theory.
Future analyses will incorporate detections from the remain-
der of the fourth observing run and subsequent observing runs
as the global detector network continues to improve in sen-
sitivity (Abbott et al. 2020b). 1 The increasing catalog of
gravitational-wave events, together with advances in model-
ing and inference techniques, will enable progressively more
stringent tests of GR in the strong-field, highly dynamical
regime.
